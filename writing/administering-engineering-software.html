Administration

Administering 15+ engineering applications I don't use myself

Part of my job is to administer the licensing and server side of more than fifteen engineering applications. They range from simulation and analysis tools to CAD, electronics, test and PLM software. The role is to keep each of them installed, licensed and working for the engineers who rely on them.

Who uses these tools

Some tools have a single user, and I support that person directly with anything that comes up in the tool. Others are used across departments, by analysis engineers, architects, designers and other specialists.

What the work involves

  • Setup. I take a new tool from vendor contact through server installation to a working, licensed service.
  • Installation documents. I write the setup instructions, and the support team installs clients from them. I keep these and the licence and server notes up to date, so anyone can pick the work up later without starting over.
  • Ongoing support. When a user hits a problem, whether with the licence or the application itself, I work out the cause and resolve it.
  • Vendor training. When a team asks for training from a vendor, I make sure it starts properly, runs smoothly and is completed. I provide the licensing and documentation it needs and fix any problems that come up during the sessions.

Where I am strongest

PLM is where my expertise is deepest, in particular ENOVIA, CATIA and DELMIA. For the other tools I work as the administrator. I know what each one is for and own its installation and licensing, and when a problem comes in I dig into that tool until I find the cause.

Why a mechanical engineering background helps

The people I support are engineers who describe problems in the terms of their own tools. My mechanical engineering degree helps me follow what they describe and ask the right follow-up questions, so we usually reach a fix faster.
