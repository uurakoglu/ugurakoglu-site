Moving manufacturing data out of Excel and into the PLM system

For a long time, our PLM process ended when the design was released. Everything after that, the manufacturing side of the data, was handled in Excel files. Between January 2023 and spring 2024 we brought that part into the same PLM platform the engineers already used, using the DELMIA module. This is what changed and what it took.

Before: a clean design, then spreadsheets

Design, analysis, configuration and project engineers already worked inside PLM. Once a design was done, production data moved into Excel. Excel is flexible, since anyone can edit anything at any time, but it does not record who changed what or when. When a changed file had already flowed into SAP, the change stayed there with no record of where it came from.

After: one place, one version

  • Traceability and permissions. Every change is recorded and every person has a defined role.
  • One live version for everyone. People see the latest data and its history, and the production side can see design data as it changes.
  • Automatic hand-off to SAP. Data leaves PLM through an integration instead of being retyped or copied. PLM and SAP always show the same data.
  • Excel is now only a report. It remains in the tracking reports inside PLM, but nobody manages data in it any more.

Changing released data on purpose

The new discipline is that released data cannot just be edited. If a change is needed, there are two legitimate routes: a normal revision, which is the right way, or a request through our ticketing system. That request goes through management approval and then to the Engineering IT team, which holds the PLM administrator rights. We make the change and also notify the relevant SAP owners, so both systems stay in step.

Bringing people along

The technical side was well defined, and most of the effort went into training and communication. Moving from Excel to PLM means trading the freedom to change anything at any moment for a system where everything is tracked and permissions are explicit. Explaining that trade, and what people gain from it, was the main work.
